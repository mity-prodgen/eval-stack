1. **At RedisVL's default threshold (cosine 0.90), an embedding-only cache served 31-70% of the queries that needed a different answer, and only 32-57% of the true paraphrases.** For 5 of 7 models, cosine scored the one-detail traps *above* real rewordings (AUC 0.29-0.45, CIs below 0.5); only voyage-4 (0.54) and the cache-tuned langcache-embed-v1 (0.56) beat chance, barely. Swapped direction is the worst case (77-92% served on 6 of 7 models; 37% on langcache-v1); swapped brand or provider is the mildest (2-14%). Raising the threshold does not fix it: A1 (the highest threshold keeping 80% of control hits) blocked only 7-19% of wrong hits while losing 9-17% of control hits. Library defaults at or below LangChain's 0.80 served 70-100% of traps.
2. **A yes/no decision-model check on top (A3) blocked 93-99% of the wrong hits and gave up at most 1% of the control hits, for about $0.006-0.015 per 1,000 lookups.** Jev 1.13, Jev 1.14 and GPT-6 Luna were within noise of each other (they accept 3.0-4.9% of the traps they see; CIs overlap). It is not a complete fix: on PAWS word-swap pairs (where "Missouri ... America" becomes "America ... Missouri"), they still let 21-25% of non-paraphrases through and turned away 4-10% of real paraphrases.
3. **The rule-based slot guard (A2) looked much better on its own author's data.** It caught 92% [88, 96] of traps on the pairs written by the same author (Claude Opus 5.5, before the pairs existed), but 71% [62, 81] on pairs written by gpt-6.1-sol and Claude Sonnet 5.5, and missed most direction traps (37% caught overall). It also blocked 14% of controls: 40% of number-format changes and 20% of other format aliases. On PAWS it blocked 6% of wrong hits. Hypotheses: direction traps did score above 0.95 on most models (6 of 7, medians 0.956-0.996); negation traps did not (only all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and BGE-M3 had medians above 0.95). The guard did block most traps and did lose hits on aliases, but how many it blocks depends on who wrote the traps.

*gemini-embedding-2 is not in these numbers yet: its free-tier quota (1,000 requests/day) stopped it at 950 of 2,006 texts.*
